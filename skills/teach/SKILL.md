# Teaching

The goal is understanding: the fact is derivable from foundations the learner already accepts, connected into their mental model, and therefore stays put. Memorized facts fade; understood facts don't.

## Why this works

Two people can give identical answers to the same questions. One holds a pile of disconnected facts; the other holds a few core truths from which those facts follow. The second one understands. Every move below builds that dependency graph: **nodes** (Principle 1) and **edges** (Principle 2).

The brain won't fully commit to a fact it isn't sure is safe: if something more fundamental might contradict it later, it hedges, and the fact never really lands. Both principles remove that risk.

## Principle 1: Unconditional truths first

Start from facts the learner can accept as-is, with no caveats or nuance. They commit instantly because nothing can come along to contradict them.

- If it needs "well, usually…", it isn't an unconditional truth yet. Dig down.
- Strong forms: **universal statements** ("all X are Y", "no X is Y"; e.g. "all communication between computers is done by sending packets") and **real definitions** (an actual definition, not a list of typical properties).
- Check that each core truth reads as obviously true to the learner before building on it.

Keep "unconditional truth" (safe to accept at face value) distinct from "axiom" (derives from nothing else). Many unconditional truths derive from deeper things; they just don't need the derivation to be safely accepted.

## Principle 2: "How could I have discovered this?"

Facts feel arbitrary when there's no visible reason they had to be that way, and the brain won't commit to arbitrary-feeling information. Make it feel discovered:

- Start from the problem: why are we even doing this?
- Motivate every intermediate step: why this formula, why this manipulation, what would lead someone to try it?
- This turns disconnected propositions into connected ones.

**Socratic or expository:** pose the problem and let the learner attempt the discovery when they can plausibly reason their way there; narrate the discovery path (3Blue1Brown style) when it's out of reach or they want it delivered.
